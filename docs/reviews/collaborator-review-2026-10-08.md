# Collaborator Review — October 8, 2026

## Title

Formology survives as a method, not a theory

## Executive summary

Formology's seven hypotheses do not hold up as stated: none of H1–H7 survives the six research tracks unchanged, and two of them (H3's "context is not form" and H6's "resolution can be reversed") fail outright on counterexamples that several fields produced independently. What survives is more useful than the axioms were. Every mechanism formology posits already has a rigorous name somewhere: "form is what stays the same" is Klein's invariant under a group, Peirce's type, Maturana's organization; "resolved by a declared context" is Tarski's interpretation, Carnap's intension and extension, Kaplan's character and content, a programming language's open term plus environment; "a resolved form is a form at the next level" is Aristotle's nesting of matter, Lawvere's functor categories, Tarski's hierarchy of metalanguages; "form is posited, not proven" is Spencer-Brown's injunction and Fichte's Setzen. No single field holds all seven, which is why "mostly a rename of X" is not the verdict for any one X. The honest verdict is "a patchwork of renames with one or two additions." The additions are the practice loop (take a case in its own language, render it as forms and contexts, compare at the level where cases become comparable, bring the finding back into the case's register) and the insistence that the reader is a context. The single most important prior-art match is a tie between two things that point the same way: Aristotle's hylomorphism with the labels swapped (formology's "form" is Aristotle's matter on the resolution axis) and model theory's interpretation of an open formula by a structure, which does everything H2 does and is cleaner about it. The recommended next step is not more theory. It is to rewrite the core as the ending core given in section 7, encode resolution as a relation rather than two disjoint classes, and run the "issue" register case under the rewritten rules looking specifically for one of the three disconfirmations that H7' now requires.

## Key findings

### 1. H3 and H6 fail outright

- **H3** ("context is not form"): Every context the treatise names is itself a form by the treatise's own test. The key signature occupies a slot, stands in for something recoverable, and means what it means only under Western notation. Confirmed by 8+ independent tracks.
- **H6** ("resolution can be reversed"): Withdrawing a context does not return the original proformation — it produces a new state that carries a trace. Erase a key signature and the accidentals still record the old key. Repeal a statute and accrued rights stand (UK Interpretation Act 1978 s.16). Confirmed by all six tracks.

### 2. H4 fails formology's own paradigm cases

Scored strictly, six of seven treatise examples fail condition (b) ("stands in for something recoverable"), including:
- The note on the staff (it *is* the pitch, not a substitute)
- The cryptographic key
- The morpheme
- The dance form

The pointing demonstrative "that," which has the least invariant content, passes all three. H4 and H1 pull in opposite directions.

### 3. H2 loses four qualifiers

- **"Complete"** → Hart's open texture: in the penumbra, the text doesn't settle the question
- **"Undetermined"** → Kaplan's character is a fully determinate function; Carnap's intension is complete
- **"Declared"** → Registers are recognized, not declared; Kaplan's context is a parameter with no declarer
- **"One bit"** → The pivot chord is doubly determined, not singly

### 4. H1 has a free parameter

"Form is what stays the same" presupposes a decision about which changes count, and that decision *is* the form. The Ship of Theseus makes it concrete. Four tracks reached this independently.

### 5. New prior art missed in the first pass

- **Model theory** (Tarski/Carnap): interpretation of an open formula by a structure — does H2 cleaner
- **Kaplan's character/content**: structurally identical to H2, with a complete function
- **PL theory open terms**: unbound variable = proformation, environment = context, thunk separates having a context from being resolved
- **Alexander's kettle**: form/context with a movable boundary — breaks H3-as-absolute
- **Eigenform** (von Foerster): objects as fixed points — form is *produced* by the operation, not read
- **Madhyamaka** (Nagarjuna): dependent origination = H4(c) as a universal; "emptiness of emptiness" = H5+H7
- **Hart's penumbra**: core/penumbra as the clearest statement of "fixed but underspecified"
- **Hollnagel's WAI/WAD**: work-as-imagined vs work-as-done — the source of the POSIWID gap, with four varieties
- **Canalisation**: a form built so context barely matters — reverses the arrow of H3

## The ending core (from section 7)

1. Form is what stays the same across a declared range of variation, in a declared respect. The range is set by the comparison level, not by the form. "Form" names what a resolution selects; it is distinct from what gets resolved.
2. A form is fixed but underspecified. Its licensed readings are settled by the contexts in force for it. No context: no reading licensed (proformation, a status relative to a level, never an exhibited item). One or more contexts: a reading under each; a form may carry several at once. Where the form is silent, resolution adds content that becomes form at the next level. A form may be prior to its context or produced by it.
3. In any one resolution there are two roles, form (what is read) and context (what it is read against). Both are filled by forms; context for a form at level N is form at level N+1 held fixed. "Context" names a role, not a kind. Direction of action is a choice of description.
4. Form iff (a') it occupies a slot where something else could have stood and (c') its meaning there is settled from outside it. Two kinds: placeholders (also stand in for a recoverable antecedent) and patterns (have at least one parameter left open). Excluded: anchors (content from themselves) and limits (nothing to read). Form-hood is relative to the resolution system in play.
5. Resolution is a relation resolvedBy(form, context); level is derived chain length; levels are supplied by cases. A resolved form is a form at the next level, possibly with emergent structure. There is no self-resolving top. The lowest levels are degenerate, not absent; any ground is posited, not reached. Disjointness of proformation/conformation holds per record, not per thing.
6. Resolution can be superseded, not undone. Where the open form and its context are stored separately, the record can be closed and the form remains; where resolution consumes the form, it cannot. Withdrawal leaves a trace; the trace is a form.
7. "There is form" is a chosen posit. Every claim built on it is disconfirmable in practice by register rejection, nesting failure, or invariance failure. Consistency confirms only non-contradiction. The intent/outcome gap belongs to systems under study, never to formology's own predictions.
8. (new) The other side of form is anchors and limits.
9. (new) Register lives in the reader's role and glossary, as a second resolution record on an already-resolved thing. Whether one form underlies several registers is decided per case by whether the shared sense does work.

## Recommended next step

Adopt the ending core in section 7. Replace the two disjoint classes Proformation and Conformation in the ontology with one relation, resolvedBy(form, context), and derive "level" as chain length. Then run the "issue" register case across four registers under the rewritten rules, hunting for one of the three practice-level failures that H7' names. If none appears, that is the first [CONFIRMED] tag the framework would earn in practice rather than by consistency.
