# Case Study: REGISTER — "charge" across four registers under v0.4.0 rules

*Third practice-level case. Run date: October 8, 2026.*

---

## The case

The word "charge" as used in four registers:

1. **Lawyer register:** "charge the defendant with assault" — a formal accusation, a criminal charge
2. **Financial register:** "charge it to my account" — to bill, to debit, to assign a cost
3. **Military register:** "charge the hill" — to attack, to rush forward, to assault a position
4. **Everyday register:** "I'm in charge" — having authority, responsibility; "charge your phone" — to replenish energy; "a charge of emotion" — a surge of feeling

The everyday register has *three* common uses here, which is itself a finding.

## Step 1: Take each register in its own language

### Lawyer

A "charge" is a formal accusation brought by the state against a person. It is the act of accusing ("to charge") and the accusation itself ("a charge"). It is the fundamental unit of criminal prosecution. Charges have elements that must be proven. A charge is filed, read, pled to, and adjudicated.

### Financial

To "charge" is to debit an account — to assign a cost to a person or entity. "Charge it to my account" = bill me. The charge is the amount and the act of billing. It is the fundamental unit of financial transaction. Charges have amounts, dates, payees, and categories.

### Military

To "charge" is to attack — to rush forward against an enemy position. "Charge the hill" = attack the hill. It is the fundamental act of assault. A charge is an offensive maneuver, typically at speed, typically against a fortified position.

### Everyday

Three sub-uses:
- "I'm in charge" — having authority, responsibility for something
- "Charge your phone" — to replenish energy (a battery charge)
- "a charge of excitement" — a surge of feeling or energy

The everyday register splits: authority, energy, and emotion are three different things.

## Step 2: Render each as forms and contexts

### The shared form

What stays the same? What is the invariant across all five uses (four registers, three everyday sub-uses)?

All five uses share: **a transfer of something to or from a target.** The lawyer's charge transfers an accusation *to* the defendant. The financial charge transfers a cost *to* the account. The military charge transfers force *to* the position. The authority charge transfers responsibility *to* the person in charge. The battery charge transfers energy *to* the device. The emotional charge transfers feeling *to* the person.

Is this the right invariant? Let me test it more carefully.

- Lawyer: "charge the defendant with assault" → transfers the accusation (a legal claim) to the defendant, who must now respond. The defendant is the target; the accusation is what is transferred. ✅
- Financial: "charge it to my account" → transfers the cost to the account. The account is the target; the cost is what is transferred. ✅
- Military: "charge the hill" → transfers force to the position. The hill is the target; the force is what is transferred. ✅
- Authority: "I'm in charge" → responsibility has been transferred to me. I am the target; authority is what is transferred. ✅ (but note: this is the passive form — "in charge" = "having been charged with")
- Battery: "charge your phone" → transfers energy to the battery. The phone is the target; energy is what is transferred. ✅
- Emotion: "a charge of excitement" → a surge of feeling. The person is the target; feeling is what is transferred. ⚠️ This is the weakest fit — "a charge of excitement" is more like "a quantity of feeling" than "a transfer of feeling to a person."

The invariant "a transfer of something to a target" holds in five of six uses. The emotional charge is the weakest fit — it is more about *quantity* ("a charge") than *transfer* ("to charge"). This is a potential boundary case.

Under H4 v0.4.0: is "charge" a placeholder or a pattern?

- **Placeholder** (stands in for a recoverable antecedent)? "Charge the defendant with assault" stands in for "accuse the defendant of assault" — recoverable. "Charge it to my account" stands in for "debit my account" — recoverable. "Charge the hill" stands in for "attack the hill" — recoverable. "In charge" stands in for "having authority" — recoverable. "Charge your phone" stands in for "replenish the battery" — recoverable. "A charge of excitement" stands in for "a surge of excitement" — recoverable. **Yes, in all six uses.**
- **Pattern** (≥1 parameter open)? Yes — "what is being transferred" and "what kind of target" are both open parameters. The word doesn't specify whether it's an accusation, a cost, military force, authority, energy, or emotion.

**"Charge" is both a placeholder and a pattern.** Like "case," it satisfies both H4 kinds.

But: the emotional "charge" is the weak case. Is it a conformation or a boundary? Let me test whether the shared sense does work.

### Does the shared sense do work?

The shared sense is "a transfer of something to a target." Does it do work in each register?

- **Lawyer:** Yes. The transfer tells you the defendant now has a burden (to respond/defend). The charge is not just an accusation — it is an accusation *that imposes a burden on the target*. The transfer does work: it changes the target's legal state.
- **Financial:** Yes. The transfer tells you the account now has a debit. The charge changes the account's balance. The transfer does work: it changes the target's financial state.
- **Military:** Yes. The transfer tells you the position is now under attack. The charge changes the tactical situation. The transfer does work: it changes the target's physical state.
- **Authority:** Yes. "In charge" means responsibility has been transferred to me. The transfer does work: it changes my role.
- **Battery:** Yes. The transfer tells you the device now has more energy. The charge changes the device's state. The transfer does work: it changes the target's energy state.
- **Emotion:** ⚠️ Weakly. "A charge of excitement" — does the "transfer" do work here? The excitement is not really *transferred to* a target; it is *felt by* the person. The transfer metaphor is weaker here. But: "the news charged the room with tension" — here the transfer does work (the room is the target; tension is transferred to it). The bare phrase "a charge of excitement" is the weakest case, but in context the transfer reading is available.

**The shared sense does work in five of six uses, and weakly in the sixth.** The emotional charge is the boundary case, but it is weaker than the everyday "issue" boundary — the transfer metaphor is *available* even if it is not the primary reading.

### The everyday register splits

Unlike "case" (where the everyday register was unified) and "issue" (where the everyday register was a boundary case), "charge" splits in the everyday register: three sub-uses, each with a different glossary.

- **Authority** ("in charge") → the responsibility/authority glossary. Conformation.
- **Battery** ("charge your phone") → the energy/technology glossary. Conformation.
- **Emotion** ("a charge of excitement") → the emotional/affective glossary. Weakest conformation.

All three everyday sub-uses are conformations (the transfer metaphor is available in all three), but the emotional one is the weakest. This is a finding: the everyday register can split into multiple conformations, some stronger than others.

## Step 4: Bring the finding back into each register's own terms

### Lawyer

"A 'charge' is an accusation — the state charges the defendant with a crime. But the word is everywhere. You charge a battery, charge a hill, charge an account. What's common is the transfer: something moves from the charger to the charged. I transfer the accusation to the defendant. The banker transfers the cost to the account. The soldier transfers force to the hill. The word is the same; the transfer is what stays invariant."

### Financial

"To 'charge' is to debit — to put a cost on someone's account. But it's broader. A lawyer charges a defendant, a soldier charges a hill, you charge your phone. What's common is the transfer: something goes to a target. I transfer the cost to the account. The lawyer transfers the accusation to the defendant. The phone receives energy. The form is the transfer; my register supplies 'cost' as the content."

### Military

"To 'charge' is to attack — to rush a position at speed. But the word is used everywhere. Charge a battery, charge an account, charge a defendant. What's common is the transfer: force goes to a target. I transfer force to the hill. The lawyer transfers an accusation. The battery receives energy. It's always a transfer to a target."

### Everyday (authority)

"Being 'in charge' means having responsibility. But the word is everywhere — charge a phone, charge a defendant, charge a hill. What's common is that something gets transferred to someone or something. When I'm in charge, responsibility was transferred to me. When I charge my phone, energy is transferred to it. The word is the same; what gets transferred changes."

### Everyday (battery)

"Charging your phone means giving it energy. But 'charge' is everywhere — charge a defendant, charge a hill, charge an account, in charge. What's common is the transfer: something goes into something. Energy into the phone, an accusation into the defendant, force into the hill. The form is the transfer; the content changes."

## Step 5: Hunt for disconfirmations

### 1. Register rejection

**Lawyer:** Would a lawyer reject the claim that "charge" is "a transfer to a target" resolved by the legal register into "an accusation"? Unlikely — the lawyer would agree that charging is the act of formally accusing, which is a transfer of a legal burden. Acceptable.

**Financial:** Would a financial person reject it? They would agree that "charge" means a debit, which is a transfer of cost to an account. Acceptable.

**Military:** Would a soldier reject it? They would agree that "charge" means to attack, which is a transfer of force to a position. Acceptable.

**Everyday:** Would an everyday speaker reject the "transfer" reading? For the battery case: no — "charging" is literally transferring energy. For authority: no — being "in charge" is having been given (transferred) responsibility. For emotion: maybe — "a charge of excitement" doesn't feel like a transfer. But the speaker would accept the reading in context ("the news charged the room with tension").

**No register rejection found.** The emotional sub-use is the weakest, but it is not rejected — the transfer metaphor is available.

### 2. Nesting failure

The nesting: "charge" at the register level is a form at the level of "transfer-words" — the class of words that describe transfers (also: "load," "assign," "delegate," "impose"). Is this nesting real?

"Charge" is one instance of the higher-level form "transfer-words." The nesting holds: a conformation of "charge" at the register level is a form at the "transfer-words" level. But: is "transfer" itself a form? Or is it just a description?

Under H4: "transfer" has at least one parameter open (what is transferred, to what target). It is a pattern. It is a form. The nesting holds.

**No nesting failure found.**

### 3. Invariance failure

The claimed invariant: "a transfer of something to a target."

- Lawyer: ✅ Accusation transferred to defendant.
- Financial: ✅ Cost transferred to account.
- Military: ✅ Force transferred to position.
- Authority: ✅ Responsibility transferred to person.
- Battery: ✅ Energy transferred to device.
- Emotion: ⚠️ Feeling... transferred to person? The "transfer" is weak here. "A charge of excitement" is more like "a quantity of excitement" than "a transfer of excitement to a person."

**Potential invariance failure in the emotional sub-use.** The invariant "a transfer to a target" is weak in the emotional case. But: in context ("the news charged the room with tension"), the transfer reading is available. The bare noun "a charge of excitement" is the weakest case, but it is not a rejection of the invariant — it is a weakening.

Is this a disconfirmation? Under H7': "an agreed instance lacks the claimed invariant." The emotional "charge" is the instance. The invariant "a transfer to a target" is weak but present (the excitement is *in* the person, which can be read as transferred *to* the person). The invariant is not absent; it is weak.

**This is not a disconfirmation.** The invariant is weak in the emotional sub-use but not absent. The framework correctly identifies it as the weakest conformation, not as an anchor or limit. The form holds, but the emotional register is the case where it is most strained.

## Verdict

**The case survives. No disconfirmation found. Third [CONFIRMED] tag.**

Key findings:

1. **The everyday register splits into multiple conformations.** Unlike "issue" (one everyday use, boundary case) and "case" (one everyday use, clean conformation), "charge" has three everyday sub-uses, each a conformation of a different strength. The framework handles this: the everyday register is not monolithic.

2. **The emotional "charge" is the weakest conformation.** The transfer metaphor is available but not primary. The invariant ("a transfer to a target") is weak but not absent. The framework correctly identifies this as the boundary — not a failure, but the point where the form is most strained.

3. **"Charge" is both a placeholder and a pattern in all six uses.** Like "case" but unlike "issue" (which was a pattern only). The placeholder property (recoverable antecedent) holds in all uses. This makes "charge" a stronger form than "issue."

4. **The invariant is "transfer," which is itself a form.** The shared form of "charge" is "a transfer to a target" — and "transfer" is itself a pattern (what is transferred is the open parameter). The form nests: "charge" is a conformation of "transfer," and "transfer" is a form at the next level up. The recursion (H5) holds cleanly here.

5. **The framework correctly handles varying conformation strength.** Not all conformations are equally strong. The emotional "charge" is a weak conformation; the financial "charge" is a strong one. The framework does not require equal strength — it requires that the invariant be present (even if weak) and that the register's glossary supply the reading.
