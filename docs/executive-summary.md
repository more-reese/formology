# Executive Summary

## Is formology additive? Yes, at two specific places.

Formology adds something no existing field does at exactly two points:

1. **The underspecification state (H8, new).** Before context is declared, form is not empty — it carries a *range* of potential determinations. Context *selects* from the range; it does not *create* meaning. This distinguishes formology from Frege (where sense-without-reference is a failure) and from linguistics (where an unresolved pro-form is simply ambiguous, not a named state). The contribution is: **underspecification is a legitimate state, not a deficiency.** This is formology's most novel claim.

2. **The application of form/context resolution beyond linguistics.** The form/context distinction (H2–H3) is well-established in linguistics (anaphora/deixis), in Frege (sense/reference), and in Aristotle (matter/form). Formology's contribution is *not* the distinction itself — it is the *application* of that distinction to domains where it has not been used: business processes (the PO case), cybernetics (VSM), music (the note/key-signature case), law (the statute/application case). The distinction is not new; the scope of its application is.

## Where is formology renaming something that exists?

| Metaphor magnitude | **Rename** of Gentner's systematicity | Gentner (1983) |

## Which hypotheses fell or changed?

- **H4 fell** as the form-ness test. It survives as the pro-form test, but it no longer defines what form *is* — only what pro-forms are.
- **H1, H2, H3, H5, H6 were revised** (active→passive, undetermined→underspecified, level-qualified, ground-level-qualified, stateless/stateful).
- **H7 survived** unchanged.
- **H8, H9, H10 are new** (underspecification as legitimate state, recursion bottoms out at non-forms, stateless/stateful resolution).

## The single most important prior-art match

**G. Spencer-Brown, *Laws of Form* (1969).** The mark is the closest existing primitive to formology's form: a single distinction that is both content and operation, recursive, and foundational. Formology diverges by separating form from context (Spencer-Brown does not) and by adding the underspecification state (Spencer-Brown's mark has no "before context" — it creates its own context). The fork is narrow and precise: formology is Spencer-Brown with a constraint (form must carry a range) and an added state (underspecification).

## Recommended next step

**Integrate with linguistics rather than parallel to it.** The survey shows formology's core (H2–H3, the form/context distinction) is well-established in linguistics, Frege, and Aristotle. The novel contribution (H8, underspecification as a legitimate state) is best developed in dialogue with computational linguistics (where underspecification is already used in parsing, but not elevated to a foundational concept). The proformation/conformation terminology should be presented as a *generalization* of anaphora resolution, not as a new field.

The most productive direction is not to claim formology as a new discipline but to **apply the form/context framework to domains where it hasn't been used** (business, cybernetics, law, music) and let the applications validate the framework. The brief's method (practice loop) is right: the value is in the doing, not in the naming.
