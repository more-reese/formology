# Reading List (v0.4.0)

*Ranked by how much of the revised core each one would have supplied on its own. Updated after the collaborator review to prioritize the sources that would have caught the most failures.*

1. **Hodges, "Model Theory," SEP** ([link](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/model-theory/)) — the H2 mechanism in full: interpretation, satisfaction, one theory many models, nonstandard models, and interpretation of one structure in another; the field formology's resolution step is a rename of.

2. **Aristotle, *Metaphysics* Z–Θ, via Ainsworth, "Form vs. Matter," SEP** ([link](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/form-matter/)) — the inverted-label match: formology's form is Aristotle's matter on the resolution axis; the levels of matter are H5 with the index built in; prime matter is the unexhibitable proformation.

3. **Peirce on types and tokens, via Wetzel, SEP** ([link](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/types-tokens/)) — H1 as type/token, the 4-place tokening relation as the resolution record, and the "car" case that forces the two-jobs split.

4. **Klein's Erlangen program, via Joyce's notes and Schiemer** ([Joyce](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/ma128/notes7.pdf)) — the formal criterion H1 lacks (invariant under a stated group), a subgroup chain as level mechanism, enlargement as reversal, and a floor and ceiling for H5.

5. **Spencer-Brown, *Laws of Form*, via Kauffman's expositions** ([link](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/Laws.pdf)) — the source of H7's injunctive method and of re-entry as recursion; the two-sided form that breaks H3; time as an alternative index to level.

6. **Alexander, *Notes on the Synthesis of Form* (1964)** — the literal form/context pair with the movable boundary (kettle/stove) that breaks H3-as-absolute and gives H5 a worked case; mutual acceptability against fixed directionality.

7. **Von Foerster, "Objects: Tokens for (Eigen-)Behaviors" (1976), via Kauffman and Stern** ([link](https://acpt.projectenportfolio.hz.nl/images/9/9d/Cybernetics_of_fixed_points.pdf)) — the mechanism for "a stable form arises," objects as fixed points, the observer in the observed, and the case where form is produced rather than read.

8. **Gentner & Clement 1988, "Evidence for Relational Selectivity"** ([link](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/newpdfpapers/GentnerClement88.pdf)) — the existing evidence for metaphor magnitude as relational depth (r ≈ .55–.65) and the separate scales that make it a profile.

9. **Hart, *The Concept of Law*, ch. VII, via the open-texture literature** — core and penumbra as the clearest statement that a form is fixed but underspecified and that resolution adds content.

10. **Kaplan, "Demonstratives," via Braun, "Indexicals," SEP** ([link](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/indexicals/)) — character/content as the closest two-stage semantics to H2, and the fork (character is a complete function; context is a parameter, not a declaration; content is not re-resolvable).
