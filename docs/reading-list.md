# Reading List

*The ten sources most worth reading next, ranked, one line each.*

1. **G. Spencer-Brown, *Laws of Form* (1969)** — The closest existing primitive to formology's form; the mark is distinction + indication + operation in one. Read the first chapter (the first 10 pages derive all of Boolean algebra from the mark).

2. **Lakoff & Johnson, *Metaphors We Live By* (1980)** — The cognitive grounding for metaphor magnitude and the fork; read the first three chapters and the discussion of "deep" vs. "surface" metaphors.

3. **Gentner, "Structure-mapping: A Theoretical Framework for Analogy" (1983)** — The formal version of formology's "fork" and "metaphor magnitude"; the relational shift is the depth-of-fork mechanism.

4. **Frege, "Über Sinn und Bedeutung" (1892)** — The sense/reference distinction that formology's form/context distinction reformulates; the case where sense-without-reference is a failure is formology's motivation for H8.

5. **Beer, *Diagnosing the System for Organizations* (1985)** — The VSM source text; the system/environment distinction (p. 7) is H3, the recursion is H5, and S1–S5 map to formology's morphology.

6. **Lyons, *Semantics* (1977), chapters on anaphora and deixis** — The linguistic foundation of H4 (pro-forms) and H2 (context resolution); the pro-form definition is the one H4 renames.

7. **Peirce, *Collected Papers* vol. 2, on type/token and the semiotic triad** — The type/token distinction is H1; the interpretant (the product of resolution) is what formology lacks and should consider adding.

8. **Kripke, *Naming and Necessity* (1980)** — Rigid designation is the ground level that H5's recursion bottoms out at; the proper-name case (Case 3) depends on Kripke's distinction between description and baptismal chain.

9. **Aristotle, *Metaphysics* Z–H** — Hylomorphism is the deepest historical prior art for form; the inversion (formology's form = Aristotle's matter) is the most philosophically productive tension in the survey.

10. **von Foerster, *Observing Systems* (1981)** — Second-order cybernetics and the observer-in-the-system; the resonant foundation for H7 and for the "practice loop" method in the research brief.
