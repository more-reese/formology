# Research Brief: Formology

*Full specification from the collaborative research pass. See README for context.*

## Origin

Formology came out of re-translating Stafford Beer's Viable System Model vocabulary morpheme by morpheme (e.g., "transduce" = trans, a boundary, plus duct, a channel). It is meant to be more foundational than VSM.

## Working Core (Hypotheses, not settled claims)

- **H1.** Form is what stays the same when the instance changes.
- **H2.** Form gets its meaning from a declared context. Before a context is declared, form is complete but undetermined (a "proformation"). Once a context is declared, it is resolved (a "conformation"). Example: a note on a staff with no key signature, then with one.
- **H3.** Context does the resolving; form is what gets resolved. Context is defined as what form is not.
- **H4.** Something counts as form only if it (a) occupies a structural slot, (b) stands in for something recoverable, and (c) gets its meaning from context rather than from itself. All three must hold.
- **H5.** Recursion: a resolved form is itself a form at the next level up. There is no ground level.
- **H6.** Resolution can be reversed by withdrawing the context.
- **H7.** Form cannot be proven, since proof requires form. It is posited, and confirmation happens in practice.

## Known Issues (from the October 7 reasoner review)

- Proformation and conformation are declared disjoint, which contradicts recursion unless "level" is added.
- Condition H4 is nearly the textbook linguistic definition of a pro-form, so "form" and "proformation" may collapse into one thing.
- The form/firm etymology in the treatise is wrong.

## Scope: Tiered by Depth of Shared Form

Starting candidates, to be promoted or demoted with stated reason:

**Likely deep:** G. Spencer-Brown's Laws of Form; linguistics (pro-forms, anaphora, deixis); Frege (sense/reference); Peirce (type/token, semiotics); Aristotle's hylomorphism; structural realism; category theory; second-order cybernetics (von Foerster, Beer); Gentner's structure-mapping theory; Lakoff and Johnson on metaphor.

**Likely middle:** programming language theory (variables, binding, scope, lazy evaluation); music theory; law (statute versus application); Christopher Alexander's pattern language; genotype and phenotype; Saussure.

**Likely shallow or uncertain:** Gestalt psychology; phenomenology; D'Arcy Thompson's morphology; Buddhist form and emptiness (rupa, sunyata).

## Shared Cases (run through every deep-tier field)

1. **REGISTER:** "issue" as used by a CEO, a developer, a lawyer, and in everyday speech.
2. **NESTING:** a case with at least three levels of resolution.
3. **NON-FORM:** find the strongest candidate for something that fails the form test.
4. **METAPHOR TRIO:** one metaphor most people find deep, one shallow, one disputed.
5. **BUSINESS:** a generic purchase order moving through a mid-sized company.
